# Linguistic evidence and limitations

Research date: 2026-10-11. Spellings are lower-cased in registry matching; original text is preserved. An abbreviation can end a real sentence: the deterministic rule is a conservative hint, not grammatical certainty. All subtitle conventions, invented source dialogue and Arabic expectations still **need a native check** before release. These sources establish spelling conventions; they do not measure dubbing quality.

## en

**Read from the files:** abbreviations and ending expressions come from `line_tidy.py` and `subs_align.py`. Their disagreements remain separate `tidy` and `subtitle` profiles. Internal English vowel/word heuristics are preserved only on English paths. Registry function words are curated, not a measured frequency ranking.

## fr

[OQLF civil-title abbreviations](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/21435/les-abreviations-et-les-symboles/les-abreviations/cas-particuliers-dabreviations/abreviation-de-madame-de-mademoiselle-et-de-monsieur) supports M., MM., Mme and Mlle. Mme/Mlle do not get an abbreviation dot. [Canadian abbreviation list](https://nos-langues.canada.ca/fr/cles-de-la-redaction/abreviations-sigles-et-symboles-liste-alphabetique) and [university writing guide](https://www.uottawa.ca/notre-universite/administration-services/services-linguistiques/guide-de-redaction) cover Dr/Dre and Pr/Pre. Dotted Dr./Prof. are compatibility candidates for regional subtitles and **need a native check**, not spellings attributed to these sources.

[OQLF spacing](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/22039/la-typographie/espacement/espacement-avant-et-apres-les-signes-de-ponctuation-et-les-symboles) and [no-break spaces](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/24566/la-typographie/espacement/contextes-exigeant-une-espace-insecable) show regional differences. The implementation accepts ordinary, no-break and narrow no-break space before marks and closing guillemets; it does not impose one typographic standard. The elision prefixes are proposed matching rules; uncommon names/apostrophes need native review. Literal apostrophes are retained in original spans.

## Matching evidence, rates and timing

**Estimated:** script-evidence guards of 50% and 80%, a minimum of four matching tokens and two distinct function words are conservative screening defaults, not calibrated probabilities. They cannot reliably distinguish similar Latin-script languages, names, code-switching or very short cues. Audio detection must decide the switch suggestion; never turn `looks_like(...)=compatible` into “verified language”. No-letter English input keeps the old unflagged result; new-language no-letter input is inconclusive.

**Read from files:** English uses 2–2.5 target Arabic words/second. **Estimated:** every new-language timing ratio starts at 1.0 so no unvalidated multiplier makes translations longer; the old “20% longer” English claim is not propagated to other languages. **Estimated:** the registry's descriptive six syllables/second placeholder is not used by translation, billing or pace measurement. No source speech rate has been measured locally. A reading-rate research PDF could not be fetched reliably, so no remembered paper numbers are presented as evidence.

To calibrate, obtain parallel, meaning-equivalent readings in each source language and MSA, including regional speakers. Measure articulated duration with the same silence rule. Compare each source→Arabic duration ratio to the English→Arabic ratio; set a robust conservative multiplier using the held-out clips. Measure actual generated duration and human judgement before changing the setting. A language's source syllables/second alone cannot justify Arabic words/second. LanguagePolicy accepts finite ratios in the estimated guard range 0.5–2.0; English prompt timing ignores overrides for exact compatibility.

**Unfinished linguistic validation:** each list has 40 curated function words, not the requested verified top-40 corpus ranking. Do not label them “most frequent” in product/research claims. [Snowball language algorithms](https://snowballstem.org/algorithms/) and [stopword ambiguity](https://snowballstem.org/texts/introduction.html) are relevant background, not a spoken-frequency ranking. A licensed, representative subtitle/dialogue corpus and native review are needed to rank them or tune language evidence. Script detection and English parity do not depend on a claimed ranking.

**Needs a native check:** all sound-description candidates, caption speaker-label/dash conventions, address/false-friend prompt notes and Arabic regex screens. Quotes/guillemets alone never prove a new speaker. Unknown parenthetical source content is retained for new languages; the English profile intentionally keeps its existing blanket removal behaviour.

### Spelling, matching and boundaries

The brief's alphabet wording needs qualification: [Taalunie, IJ alphabetisation](https://taaladvies.net/ij-alfabetisering/) treats ij as i+j in alphabet order, with no separate alphabet entry, while both characters are capitalised in IJsselmeer. Keep IJ distinct from Y. Compounds remain whole; the tokenizer is not a morphological parser. [Official technical spelling guide, 2016](https://woordenlijst.org/img/Technische_Handleiding.pdf), section 10.1, supports the title/abbreviation family. [Academic titles](https://taaladvies.net/dr-ir-mr/) confirms that mr. is a legal qualification, not the English Mister. No automatic expansion or title deletion is implemented.

Reduced forms m'n, z'n, d'r and personal short forms are **cited** in [Taalunie pronoun advice](https://taaladvies.net/volle-en-gereduceerde-vormen-van-persoonlijke-en-bezittelijke-voornaamwoorden-algemeen/). `'t`, `'s avonds`, `'n`, `zo'n`, `ie` are matching/input candidates, **needs a native check** for coverage and natural subtitle use. Internal m/z/d/zo prefixes split matching tokens; leading apostrophes are preserved punctuation tokens, not speech loss. Both straight and curved apostrophes preserve original spans. Rejoining originals is **proven**, including combining characters, by fixture/offset tests; matching is not a spoken-form rewrite.

Decimal comma, thousands dot/space and ordinals 1e/1ste/2de are spelling/input conventions. [Official numeral advice](https://www.vlaanderen.be/team-taaladvies/spellingregels/aaneenschrijven/aaneenschrijven-07-getallen-telwoorden-en-breuken) supports ordinal forms. No number value, thousands grouping or AM/PM is inferred by the tokenizer. A multiword sentence ending in etc./enz. can end; internal enumeration dots remain protected before lowercase continuation. This is an **estimated punctuation heuristic**, not syntax proof. Titles at true sentence ends remain conservatively ambiguous. Ellipsis stops in tidy, trails off in subtitle profile; these are app policies, not Dutch grammar claims. Quotes accepted include straight, modern curly, low-high and guillemets; quotation alone never identifies a speaker.

### Meaning, gender and subtitle practice

**Cited:** [formal/informal address advice](https://www.vlaanderen.be/intern/werkplek/ondersteuning/heerlijk-helder/u-of-je), [pronoun guidance](https://onzetaal.nl/taalloket/persoonlijk-voornaamwoord), and [ANS verb groups](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans18050101lingtopic) / [end-group order](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans18050701lingtopic). Verbs/adjective forms do not identify the speaker's natural sex; third-person/possessive pronouns can identify their referents. Zij/ze can be plural. U need not be plural; je can be generic. Belgian gij/ge does not by itself prove formal address. Context and explicit addressee facts decide Arabic agreement. Gender of de/het and a job noun is not person sex. Preserve separable-verb meaning (bel ... op) and verb-final dependencies: subtitle wrapping is not a safe semantic cut. Exact fixture naturalness and edge cases **need a native check**. One nl entry with empty variants loses regional labelling; it does not standardise speakers' accents.

[Publisher's time lesson](https://utalk.com/en/news/how-to-tell-the-time-in-dutch) supports half drie = 02:30/14:30, kwart over = quarter past, kwart voor = quarter to. Those values are **cited**, not inferred dubbing measurements; no AM/PM without context. Eventueel in an optional/if-needed sense is supported by [Taalunie contextual advice](https://taaladvies.net/desgevallend-of-indien-nodig/). [ANW actueel](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/actueel) and [ANW lekker](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/lekker) have sense descriptions. The opened ANW entries for brutaal, gezellig and mooi exposed grammatical data rather than complete sense descriptions: meanings used in invented cases are **provisional**, needs a native check. All ten idiom/time/false-friend Arabic proposals and screens need Ali's approval. No one-word translation is guaranteed across contexts.

The [Dutch timed-text guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215350158-Dutch-Timed-Text-Style-Guide), last change-log entry **19 December 2025**, supports hyphen-marked dual speakers and bracketed sound descriptions. Its current output quotation preference is not a reason to reject other input quotes. The registered `[muziek]`, `(zucht)`, `(lacht)`, `[geluid]`, `[applaus]` are conservative candidates, not an exhaustive standard; **needs a native check**. Unknown parentheticals are retained rather than silently deleting dialogue. Music-mark cues are omitted by the existing policy.

**Read from the files:** exactly 40 distinct curated function words, not frequency-ranked; beta false, variants empty, same-as-English price policy. **Estimated:** timing ratio 1.0; Arabic word budget unchanged. **Unmeasured:** source speaking rate (None). ### Orthography and matching

[Unicode normalisation specification](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr15/) describes decomposition, not language-aware transliteration. **Proven here** with Python normalisation/matching tests: ł/Ł survive the previous fold; the Polish matching branch explicitly maps them to l/L. ą ć ę ń ó ś ź ż fold for matching only. łódź→lodz, Łukasz→Lukasz, wąż→waz, żółć→zolc; other languages and the default still preserve ł/Ł. Original text/offsets survive exactly. Seven-case paradigms are **cited** in [Polish Academy dictionary editorial principles, 2025](https://pliki.wsjp.pl/zasady_opracowania_wsjp.pdf). Marek/Marka/Markiem are an inflection illustration, **needs a native check**; the tokenizer neither stems nor equates them. Names require contextual/glossary identity, not guessed suffix removal.

The [Polish Language Council consolidated spelling rules](https://rjp.pan.pl/app/uploads/2025/11/2-zalacznik-do-komunikatu-11-25-wersja-jednolita.pdf), effective January 2026, sections 6 and 11.3, support dotless dr/mgr where abbreviated last letters match and dotted inflected forms in appropriate contexts; female dr can remain dotless. A period can serve both abbreviation and sentence end. zł is dotless. This package recognises input forms, never changes their spelling or expands them.

Integer+dot may be an ordinal; decimals use comma, thousands can use space/dot. Numbered endings, dates with year+r., and final titles are ambiguous: `1.`, `w 2026 r.`, and `godz. 9.` do not prove a stop. Multiword enumeration endings itd./itp. can stop, or continue before lowercase. This conservative **estimated** policy can miss a true end; it must not generate new timings or change Arabic. Low-high quotes `„ ”`, guillemets and hyphen/en/em dash input are retained; the old German low-high pair is untouched.

### Gender, address and subtitle practice

[Dictionary zrobić paradigm](https://wsjp.pl/haslo/podglad/15083/zrobic/4988374/co-zrobiles) is **cited** evidence for past person/gender forms: self-reference zrobiłem/zrobiłam, addressee zrobiłeś/zrobiłaś, and third-person referents. Subject pronouns can be absent, so identify the subject using morphology and context. Quoted/reported forms do not establish the current speaker's sex; mobile person endings and conditionals need contextual review. No articles or subject-pronoun count is used as proof of bad Polish. Oni/masculine-personal plural versus one/nonvirile is not a mechanical men-versus-women split: nonhuman groups belong to the latter too. Use actual referents for Arabic human/nonhuman agreement. These are prompt instructions, **not a deterministic morphological tagger**; complete source naturalness and Arabic output need native check.

[University language advice](https://www.poradnia-jezykowa.uni.lodz.pl/szczegoly/mowienie-o-sobie-w-trzeciej-osobie) / [polite address advice](https://www.poradnia-jezykowa.uni.lodz.pl/szczegoly/zwroty-grzecznosciowe-w-pracy) support third-person pan/pani/państwo address. Ty/wy are informal singular/plural; respectful singular need not become Arabic plural. Państwo may mean a state rather than an addressee. [Doctor entry](https://wsjp.pl/haslo/podglad/7920/doktor/1203410/lekarz) includes pani doktor: masculine job noun does not turn the woman into a man. Arabic title/pronoun proposals remain **needs a native check**.

The [Polish timed-text guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/216787928-Polish-Timed-Text-Style-Guide), last change-log entry **17 October 2025**, supports low-high quotation, hyphen+space dual speakers and bracketed descriptions. `[muzyka]`, `(wzdycha)`, `(śmiech)`, `[hałas]`, `[oklaski]` are candidate input filters, **needs a native check**. No exhaustive sound-word list is claimed.

**Read from files:** 40 distinct curated words, beta false, variants empty and same price policy. **Estimated:** timing ratio 1.0 and six-second gold trial slots; these are not measured reading/speaking rates. **Unmeasured:** speaking rate None. Obtain labelled quiet/noisy, fast/slow, gender/person/polite/quoted dialogue, names in several cases, numbers/dates, whispers and overlapping speakers, then measure original and Arabic durations before timing changes. Eight invented past predicate-gender cases deliberately omit speaker metadata, two test raw addressee vowel marks. Arabic first-person verbs such as فعلتُ alone do not encode male/female: do not demand a nonexistent distinction. Every Arabic proposal/regex is provisional. No copied-voice native-accent guarantee.
